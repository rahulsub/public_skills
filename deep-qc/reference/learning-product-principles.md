# Learning-product principles (distilled from expert reviewer feedback)

Domain defaults for reviewing children's learning products, distilled from
~30 rounds of feedback by learning-science reviewers on a real K-8 reading
course. **A project's own rulebook overrides these** — but when a project has
no rulebook yet, these are the standards expert reviewers applied, and a
review that ignores them will re-learn them the slow way.

## Load and pacing

- **Count every discrete answer moment**, not "questions". A lesson reviewers
  called "over a dozen questions" measured 55. Compare units against each
  other; the lightest units were also the best-taught, so cuts are usually free.
- "We've overdone questions to the detriment of the mental model" — checks that
  interrupt every paragraph become compliance theater. Reading needs stretches
  that are just reading.
- Every screen needs an active interaction — but an interaction is not
  automatically a question. Retells that are actually clozes, and "your turn"
  beats that are actually MCQs, count as asks.

## Question construction

- **No yes/no questions** — a two-option item is a coin flip.
- **Four options minimum**, all plausible, none a joke; the key must not be the
  only option differing in length, style, specificity, or register
  ("the correct answer unlike the others is the obvious one").
- **No stem echo**: the key must not restate the question's own words.
- **Feedback teaches without revealing**, and never references content the
  learner has not reached yet (spoilers in wrong-answer feedback are the
  reviewer-quoted classic).
- **Feedback lands directly under the thing acted on** ("MCQ feedback should be
  directly under the ABCD").
- Questions are bespoke to the text — generic strategy advice ("find the one
  sentence the story is about") is banned.

## Vocabulary

- **Production over recognition**: the learner says the word, then produces
  meaning in their own words; a parroted definition is a miss — but a rubric
  must never reject a correct answer for using a morpheme the card itself
  taught.
- **One definition per word, verbatim everywhere** it appears (card, glossary,
  feedback, goal). Definition drift is a repeat reviewer complaint.
- **Minimal pairs**: example and non-example differ in exactly one
  definition-critical attribute, each with a why.
- **Syllables are not morphemes**: first pronunciation breaks by syllable
  (dictionary style); meaning-parts are a separate step, and only real
  morphology — never invented splits.
- Words stamped by a video or spec as gloss-only stay out of the taught slot.

## Instruction shape

- **Gradual release is one process practiced three ways** — model it (showing
  the thinking, including candidates considered and rejected: "don't model the
  thinking as if it's easy"), do it together, then do it alone. The independent
  turn must require transfer, not repetition — and must not be pre-answered by
  the guided turn.
- **Evidence first, own words second** — never "copy the sentence" ("we're
  teaching them to plagiarize").
- **Set a purpose before reading; activate prior knowledge; never re-teach as
  if new.** Scaffolds fade across a unit.
- A wrong answer holds the gate, explains why, and never reveals the key; a
  release after N tries is named as moving on, never dressed as a win.
- The guide adds; it never restates the printed question (the on-screen form of
  "too much narration").

## Presentation

- Screen real estate mostly devoted to the thing the learner must attend to —
  the reviewers' single most repeated ask. See `attention-standard.md`.
- Reading should feel like a book: chrome recedes, text is the page's mass,
  checks sit beside their own passage.
- Adult metadata (timers, counters, version strings, scripts) never reaches
  the learner.
- Register: plain, warm, no jargon (procedure names are for builders), no
  hedging idioms above the reading band, no ALL-CAPS emphasis, US spelling for
  US audiences, glossary support for hard words on first read.

## Process expectations reviewers stated outright

- **"Most of it is a direct transfer from the documents we gave you"** —
  content comes from the source spec; extrapolation where transfer was expected
  is the fastest way to lose a reviewer.
- **Human review before reviewer review** — obvious defects reaching the
  customer "wastes our time and you get the same feedback over and over".
- **Benchmark real products** in the category for hours, not screenshots.
- **Small targeted checkers beat one omniscient reviewer** — one lens per
  concern.
- Ask reviewers for **specific parameters** (question volume, narration length)
  once, encode them as thresholds, and stop re-litigating taste.
